## Task 1: Measure manual containment baseline

In this task, you will establish a baseline for how long manual containment would take if a human analyst had to identify the containment action without the automation built in Challenge 3.

1. Select a representative noisy administrative enumeration incident generated by **ZAVA-Noise-Administrative-Enumeration** and review its evidence, entities, alert details, and timeline at a high level.
2. Start your manual baseline timer before you begin analysis. Use the staged helper script **C:\LabFiles\Scripts\Start-ZavaManualContainmentTimer.ps1** if it is available on the VM, or record a UTC start timestamp in your notes. A convenience copy may also be available on the Public Desktop.
3. Determine the containment action a human analyst would recommend for this incident. Do not modify the NSG during this baseline; the goal is to measure decision time, not to perform containment.
4. Stop the timer when you can state the recommended action, affected entity, and reason the incident is noisy rather than malicious. Use **C:\LabFiles\Scripts\Stop-ZavaManualContainmentTimer.ps1** if available, or record a UTC stop timestamp in your notes.
5. Capture the following manual baseline fields for later workbook reporting: incident number, incident title, analytics rule name, analyst start time, analyst stop time, elapsed seconds, decision, account, source IP, and why the incident is a false positive.

> [!Tip]
> Manual baseline time is a process metric. It should reflect how long it takes an analyst to reach a safe containment recommendation from the incident evidence, not how long it takes to click through every available screen.

## Task 3: Tune the noisy administrative enumeration rule

In this task, you will reduce a known false-positive pattern without disabling the rule or weakening unrelated detections.

1. In Microsoft Defender portal, use **Microsoft Sentinel > Configuration > Analytics** and open the active scheduled analytics rule **ZAVA-Noise-Administrative-Enumeration**. Review its current KQL query, entity mappings, schedule, alert threshold, and incident creation setting.
2. Tune the rule query by excluding exactly the benign account **svc-zava-audit@zavacorp.example** and source IP **198.51.100.23**.
3. Preserve the rule status as enabled. Do not disable **ZAVA-Noise-Administrative-Enumeration** and do not suppress the rule wholesale.
4. Use a narrow exclusion that removes only records where the account is **svc-zava-audit@zavacorp.example** and the source IP is **198.51.100.23**.
5. Keep the rule query time-bounded and ensure the query still returns **TimeGenerated**, account, source IP, host or VM, and any custom fields needed for entity mapping and investigation.
6. Test the updated query with current data before saving the rule. Confirm that benign administrative enumeration rows tied to **svc-zava-audit@zavacorp.example** and **198.51.100.23** are excluded while other suspicious administrative enumeration records can still match.
7. Save the tuned rule and record the timestamp of the tuning change.

> [!Important]
> This challenge validates tuning quality. A disabled rule is not considered tuned. The rule **ZAVA-Noise-Administrative-Enumeration** must remain enabled and its query must contain both exact exclusions: **svc-zava-audit@zavacorp.example** and **198.51.100.23**.

## Task 4: Prove detection coverage remains intact

In this task, you will prove that tuning noise did not break the true-positive storyline or the learner-authored failed-logon detection.

1. Compare incident or alert volume before and after the tuning timestamp for the noisy administrative enumeration pattern.
2. Use KQL to demonstrate that the excluded benign source decreases after the rule update.

```kusto
let tuningTime = datetime(null);
ZavaSOCSeed_CL
| where TimeGenerated > ago(7d)
| where ScenarioType_s has "Administrative" or NoiseGroup_s has "Administrative"
| extend Period = iff(isnull(tuningTime) or TimeGenerated < tuningTime, "Before tuning", "After tuning")
| summarize Events=count() by Period, BenignAccount_s, BenignSourceIp_s
| order by Period asc, Events desc
```

3. Replace **datetime(null)** in your working copy with the actual UTC tuning timestamp you recorded. Keep the saved workbook query parameterized or clearly labeled so another analyst understands the before-and-after split.
4. Confirm that seeded true-positive rule **ZAVA-TruePositive-Suspicious-VM-Access-And-NSG-Change** remains enabled and its associated incident evidence is still available.
5. Confirm that learner-created rule **ZAVA-Learner-Failed-VM-Logons** remains enabled and is still capable of producing a recent incident from fresh failed-logon activity.
6. Use KQL to summarize active coverage for the rules that must remain intact.

```kusto
SecurityIncident
| where TimeGenerated > ago(7d)
| summarize arg_max(TimeGenerated, *) by IncidentNumber
| where Title has_any ("ZAVA-TruePositive-Suspicious-VM-Access-And-NSG-Change", "ZAVA-Learner-Failed-VM-Logons", "ZAVA-Noise-Administrative-Enumeration")
| project IncidentNumber, Title, CreatedTime, LastModifiedTime, Severity, Status, RelatedAnalyticRuleIds
| order by CreatedTime desc
```

7. Record the evidence that supports all three conclusions: noise is reduced, the true-positive detection remains preserved, and the failed-logon detection remains preserved.
